Open MoonlightNext by itself and you get the device window. The machines are on the left; the one selected is on the right.

![The device window](./images/devices.png)

## The list

The dot before a name is green when the machine answers. Under the name is its system and, while it has a stream, "streaming".

**+** adds a device, **−** removes the one selected, after asking: its stream, open or warm, ends with it, and where its window was is forgotten. Its pairing stays, so adding it again needs no new PIN.

The **…** menu has **Settings…**, **Pair This Device…** and **Classic Moonlight…**, which opens Moonlight's own screens.

## The picture

The picture is what is on the machine's screen now. It is fetched when you select a device and when you come back to the window, with a spinner while it loads. The round arrow fetches a new one.

A device has a picture only if you give it a **Screenshot** command: a shell command, run on your Mac, that writes an image to its standard output. Two that work:

```sh
# A Mac you can ssh into (wakes its display first)
ssh my-mac "caffeinate -u -t 5 >/dev/null 2>&1 & sleep 0.8; screencapture -x -t jpg /tmp/s.jpg && cat /tmp/s.jpg"

# A Linux machine on Wayland (wlroots, Hyprland) you can ssh into
ssh me@my-pc "XDG_RUNTIME_DIR=/run/user/1000 WAYLAND_DISPLAY=wayland-1 grim -t jpeg -q 70 -"
```

A black picture means the machine's screen is asleep: have the command wake it first, as the Mac one above does. A locked machine shows its lock screen.

## The status line

Under the name: whether it answers and how quickly, and whether a stream is open on it, to this Mac or to another.

## How a device is streamed

Changes are saved as you make them and hold from the next Connect.

| Setting | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Name** | What it is called here, and in [links](/links). No two devices have the same name, whatever the capitals: one that is taken is not accepted, and the name stays as it was. Nor is a new name while the device has a stream, open or warm: end it first. |
| **Address** | Where it is: an IP address or a name. |
| **System** | macOS or Linux. Decides the mark on the stream's bar. |
| **Stream size** | **Follows the window**: the stream has the window's pixels and restarts when you resize it; the two numbers are the window to open, in points. **Fixed, scaled to fit**: the stream keeps the size you give, in pixels, and is scaled into the window with black bands where the shapes differ. Nothing is stretched. Either way a width from 320 to 8192 and a height from 200 to 8192; other numbers are not kept. |
| **True Pixels** | The stream has as many pixels as the monitor's panel, not as many as macOS draws. On for a new device: on a monitor run at a scaled resolution, a fullscreen stream without it asks the host for more pixels than the panel can show (6400 × 2700 for a 5120 × 2160 panel), and a small host cannot keep up. See [the stream window](/stream-window#true-pixels). |
| **Raw colours** | Show the machine's colour values as they are. Without it macOS fits them to your display, which on a wide-gamut monitor makes a desktop look washed out next to its usual self. |
| **Instant pointer** | Your Mac draws the pointer itself, so it moves with your hand instead of one round trip later. The host must leave its own cursor out of the picture: a Mac does with the cursor helper, and you see its cursor shapes; a Hyprland PC does with `pc-wake`, and you see a plain arrow whatever is under it. See [Hosts](/hosts). |
| **No sound** | The device's sound is not played here, and your Mac's sound output is not opened for the stream. For a machine that sends none (Sunshine with `stream_audio = disabled`), or whose sound reaches you another way. A stream started while your Mac's screen is locked starts without sound whatever this says, because the output cannot be opened then; when you unlock, it connects again by itself, with sound. |
| **Bitrate** | In kbps. Empty takes the bitrate of Settings › Quality; with that on automatic too, the app chooses by the stream's size. |
| **Screenshot** | The command for the picture, above. |
| **Before connecting** | A shell command run on your Mac (by `/bin/sh -c`, with nothing to type into) before a stream is started, to wake or unlock the machine. It has eight seconds; the stream is started after that whether or not it is done or went well. Bringing a stream that is already there forward does not run it. If the last line it prints is an address, the stream goes there instead of to **Address**. It is run with `MOONLIGHT_POINTER` set to 1 or 0: whether **Instant pointer** is on. |
| **Clipboard** | An ssh destination for the machine (`me@my-pc`, or a name from your ssh config). With it, the two machines share a clipboard: see below. With a Mac it is also how the instant pointer's cursor shapes arrive: over ssh, and not by that Mac's helper connecting to yours, which macOS there may refuse. |

## Connect

**Connect** starts the stream in a window of its own, where that device's window was the last time and as large as you left it. If the monitor it was on is gone, it opens in the middle of the screen.

The moment you connect, a window appears there with the device's last screenshot, dimmed, and a spinner. A new screenshot is fetched meanwhile and shown if it comes first; the stream takes the window's place when its first picture arrives, a second or three later. While a device has a stream, the button reads **Show Window** and brings that window forward, and **End Stream** beside it ends the stream for good. If you connect to a device whose stream you closed a moment ago and which is still shutting down, the new stream starts as soon as the old one has gone.

**Check Link** has [the doctor](/settings#doctor) look at the way to this device and say what it can carry.

Closing the device window does not end any stream. While the app runs, its Dock icon's menu (right-click it) lists your devices, and says which have a stream: choosing one connects to it, or brings its stream forward.

## A shared clipboard

The stream itself carries no clipboard, so MoonlightNext passes it over ssh, to the destination in the device's **Clipboard** field. Your Mac must be able to ssh there without being asked for a password.

- What you copied on your Mac goes to the other machine when you switch to its stream.
- What you copied there comes back when you switch away from the stream.

So copy on one side, switch, paste on the other. It takes about a tenth of a second. Text up to 1 MB, pictures up to 20 MB (a screenshot copied on one side pastes on the other), and files copied in the Finder or a file manager, up to 64 of them and 200 MB together: they are unpacked into a cache folder at the other end and put on its clipboard as files. With a folder or a link among them, two of the same name, or more than that, none of them goes. A password manager's copies marked as concealed are not passed on, in either direction. A Mac is read and written with `pbpaste` and `pbcopy` (and `osascript` for pictures and files), a Linux machine with `wl-paste` and `wl-copy` (the `wl-clipboard` package, on Wayland; `python3` for files). Between two Macs signed in to the same Apple account, macOS's own Universal Clipboard does much of this by itself.
